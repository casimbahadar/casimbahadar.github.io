Automating repetitive work

Scripts and small tools that take over the copying, sorting, renaming, reminding and report-building someone currently does by hand. Where it helps, this can include AI steps such as summarizing documents or drafting replies for a person to check before anything is sent.

Good for
Anyone spending hours a week repeating the same steps in email, spreadsheets or folders.
What you get
A tool that runs the task, a record of what it did, and a short guide to running or pausing it.
Related work
The email to job ticket demo has an AI model draft tickets from customer emails, with code checking every field against the email. The workflow time audit shows which steps of a repeated task take the most hours a year. The invoice and bank reconciliation demo matches payments to invoices and leaves a short list of what needs a person.

Web apps and internal tools

Forms, trackers, booking and inventory tools, dashboards and customer portals that replace paper, email threads or an overgrown spreadsheet. They run in any browser and can be added to a phone's home screen like an app.

Good for
Teams whose process lives in someone's inbox or a shared spreadsheet.
What you get
An app shaped around your steps, tested on the devices your team uses, with notes on where the data is stored and who can see it.
Related work
Ludo Mehfil syncs online rooms through a shared database. WayPack installs to the home screen and keeps cloud saves for each player.

Connecting the tools you already use

Getting your forms, spreadsheets, calendars, databases and other services to pass information to each other, so nobody retypes it and nothing falls between them.

Good for
Businesses running on several apps that don't share information.
What you get
The connection built through each service's official access, with notes on what to check if one of those services changes.
Related work
The job request intake demo turns one form entry into a calendar file, a customer message and a spreadsheet row. It stops short of connecting to live services, which is the part a real project adds.

Cleaning up and reporting on data

Turning messy exports and years of spreadsheets into clean, consistent records, then building the reports and charts you need. My accounting and audit background helps here.

Good for
Anyone who can't answer a basic question about their business without an afternoon in Excel.
What you get
Cleaned data, the steps to repeat the cleanup, and reports you can rerun when new data comes in.
Related work
The customer list cleanup demo fixes a messy spreadsheet in the browser and logs every change it makes.

Websites

Trades, shops, studios and community groups that need a clear, current site.
What you get
The finished site, your domain connected, hosting set up in your name, and a short guide for updating text yourself.

Training, courses and interactive pieces

Interactive lessons with quizzes and saved progress for onboarding staff or teaching customers, and browser games or demos for events, classrooms and promotions.

Related work
Step Through, AI Learner's Permit, Cadence and the rest of my work page.

Not on this list?

Describe it anyway. The first call is for working out whether software is the right fix and what the smallest useful version looks like. If it isn't something I should build, I'll say so and point you somewhere better.

How a project runs

Each project gets a fixed quote after the first call.

  1. A short call

    You describe the problem. I ask what happens today, who will use the result, and what finished looks like to you.

  2. A written scope

    One page listing what I'll build, what's out of scope, the price and the delivery date.

  3. Building, with previews

    You get a link you can open on your own phone as the work progresses, so feedback happens early.

  4. Handover

    The code and hosting are in your name. I walk you through the changes you can make yourself.

Where my background helps

I worked in insurance claims intake and in accounting and audit before moving into development. If your project involves intake forms, records or step-by-step procedures, I already know the questions that catch missing information early.

I use AI coding tools to work faster. I plan each project, test it myself on real devices, and I'm the person you deal with from the first call to handover.

What I don't take on

Anything built to break into systems, collect people's information without their consent, send spam, or get around another service's rules. Tools that handle personal or financial information get the access controls and data handling they need before launch.
